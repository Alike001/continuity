## Weak criteria to address in the presentation

1. Product usefulness needs outside validation. Do not imply developer demand beyond the official scaffold gap. The strongest honest claim is that the missing recovery mechanism is verified, while first-user demand remains unproven.
2. Technical execution has one explicit evidence gap. Stale and fork rejection pass deterministic tests, but they did not produce live Coston2 receipts. Show the real recovery, continuation, replay, ciphertext, and terminal-error evidence first.
3. Clarity and future potential need a credible next step. The immediate roadmap is to package the protocol as an extension SDK and orchestrator, add event-indexed reads, and test it with a second stateful FCC application. Avoid claiming automatic failover or production confidentiality today.
4. The 30-second setup rule is not fully met for a new operator. Judges can inspect the recorded run immediately, but reproducing the FCC deployment requires local machines, proxy configuration, and tunnels.

## Competitive reading

Past Flare winners usually made one native primitive visible in an end-to-end transaction, but often had thin tests, manual operations, or unclear trust boundaries. Continuity is competitive on Flare-native depth, recovery mechanics, tests, and inspectable evidence. It is weaker than the strongest current Summer Signal entries on public availability, hardware TEE proof, external users, and one-click reproducibility.
